## 1. Objective

To assess, in people with Parkinson disease (PD), whether the side with lower wrist-IMU arm swing agrees with the clinically more affected upper limb during self-paced walking; how this clinically aligned asymmetry changes in fast walking and other functional walking tasks; and whether the magnitude and direction of sensor asymmetry persist after about six months.

## 2. Design and transparency

- Retrospective secondary analysis of the public WearGait-PD data.
- The research question was chosen after literature and data feasibility checks.
- Full-sample results for clinical–sensor agreement or task differences were not inspected before this plan was finalized.
- The Version 1 (V1) standard-walking (SP) primary analysis and the SP–fast-walking (HP) secondary analysis after this plan are treated as limited confirmatory analyses.
- Walking with turns (SPmT), Timed Up and Go (TUG), tandem walking, subgroup analyses and alternative measures are labelled exploratory.

## 3. Data sources

- WearGait-PD V1 cross-sectional: Synapse syn55052683
- WearGait-PD V2 longitudinal CSV: Synapse syn74686431
- Dataset descriptor: Anderson et al., Scientific Data 2026;13:440, doi:10.1038/s41597-026-06806-2
- Annotation and IMU sampling: 100 Hz; IMU angular velocity in rad/s

## 4. Analysis populations

### 4.1 V1 primary population

Participants with PD who meet all of the following:

1. Bilateral three-axis wrist gyroscope data in the SP file.
2. MDS-UPDRS items 3.3 RUE/LUE, 3.4 R/L, 3.5 R/L and 3.6 R/L all available.
3. Right and left upper-limb sums not tied.
4. Part III clinical examination on the same day as the sensor assessment.
5. At least four eligible arm swings detected on each side.

Expected N before algorithm eligibility: about 70. Post-analysis exclusions follow these rules only and are not changed according to the direction of results.

### 4.2 V1 sensitivity populations

- Clinical examination within 30 days
- All eligible participants regardless of examination date
- Paired sample with both SP and HP

### 4.3 V2 longitudinal population

- Wrist data at both sessions for SP: up to 44 participants
- Tandem at both sessions: up to 43 participants
- Clinically aligned longitudinal analyses use baseline clinical laterality only and are not interpreted as changes in clinical laterality between visits.

## 5. Clinical laterality

For each side: `UpperMotorSide = Rigidity (3.3 upper extremity) + Finger tapping (3.4) + Hand movements (3.5) + Pronation–supination (3.6)`.

- R > L: right more affected; L > R: left more affected; R = L: tie.
- Tremor items are excluded from the primary definition because they can overlap directly with the wrist gyroscope signal.

## 6. Wrist arm-swing measures

### 6.1 Intervals

- SP, HP, SPmT, TUG: `GeneralEvent == Walk`; tandem: `GeneralEvent == TandemWalk`.
- Continuous intervals of at least 2.0 s; swings detected in all eligible intervals of a file are pooled.
- Turns, standing, sit-to-stand and stand-to-sit are excluded.

### 6.2 Signal processing

1. X/Y/Z angular velocity of each wrist.
2. Second-order zero-phase Butterworth low-pass filter, 3 Hz.
3. For each wrist, three-axis PCA fitted to all eligible straight-walking samples of a file; the first component is used as swing angular velocity.
4. The first component is integrated to angle, and drift is removed by subtracting a 1-s symmetric moving average.
5. Periodicity check in the 0.3–3 Hz band.
6. Swings retained with peak prominence of at least 2°, amplitude of at least 5° and peak angular velocity of at least 10°/s.
7. Median peak angular velocity and median amplitude for each wrist and task.

### 6.3 Pre-specified measures

- Primary sensor measure: median peak angular velocity (deg/s)
- Secondary sensor measure: median swing amplitude (degrees)
- `Sensor log ratio = log(R/L)`
- Clinically aligned value: with M the more and L the less affected side, `Aligned asymmetry = log(less affected / more affected)`; positive values indicate smaller swing of the clinically more affected arm.

## 7. Hypotheses and statistical analysis

### Primary H1: direction agreement in SP

- Agreement between the side with lower median peak angular velocity and the clinically more affected side.
- Estimate: agreement proportion with exact 95% CI.
- Inference: two-sided exact binomial test, H0 = 0.50, alpha = 0.05.
- Supporting: Cohen kappa with 95% CI.
- Success criterion: lower limit of the 95% CI of agreement above 0.50, with the effect in the clinically expected direction.

With an expected N of 70 and a true agreement of about 67.5%, power is about 83%. Smaller agreement will be estimated precisely, and non-significance will not be interpreted as proof of disagreement.

### Confirmatory secondary H2: change from SP to HP

- Outcome: aligned asymmetry based on median peak angular velocity.
- Paired participant-level SP–HP difference with 95% CI.
- Paired t test if the distribution is adequate; the pre-specified Wilcoxon signed-rank test if there is marked non-normality or influential points. The choice is recorded as a blinded distributional diagnostic.
- Alpha = 0.05; a single pre-specified secondary hypothesis, labelled separately from the primary.

### Exploratory task generalization

- Aligned asymmetry in SP, HP, SPmT, TUG and tandem compared with a linear mixed model with a participant random intercept; task as a categorical fixed effect.
- Contrasts against SP adjusted with the Benjamini–Hochberg false discovery rate.
- Gait-speed adjustment may remove part of the task effect and is therefore a sensitivity model, not the main model.
- Because task order was fixed, task contrasts are not described as pure difficulty or causal effects.

### V2 six-month stability

- ICC(A,1) with 95% CI for R, L, sensor log ratio and aligned asymmetry in SP.
- Agreement and kappa of the sensor lower-swing side between the two visits.
- Systematic change as a paired estimate with CI.
- The same analysis for tandem walking as exploratory.
- Described as six-month stability rather than test–retest reliability.

## 8. Sensitivity analyses

1. Clinical date: same day (primary), within 30 days, all eligible.
2. Clinical laterality: rigidity only, bradykinesia only.
3. Sensor outcome: peak angular velocity (primary), amplitude (secondary).
4. PCA: three-axis (primary); two-axis PCA in the non-longitudinal plane according to the official orientation.
5. Quality: with and without first-component variance share ≥0.50 and periodic power ≥0.75.
6. Exclusion of participants with DBS.
7. Direction by site and leave-one-site-out.
8. Cases with very small bilateral sensor differences are not removed; they are interpreted together with continuous aligned asymmetry.

## 9. Missing data, outliers and transformations

- Complete-case exclusion from an analysis when clinical data or the bilateral primary wrist measure are missing.
- No imputation for the primary outcome.
- No outcome-based outlier removal beyond the algorithm thresholds.
- If a log ratio cannot be computed because a value is zero, the raw signal and extraction are checked; no constant is added post hoc, and the value is treated as missing with the reason recorded.
- Influential points are examined with a leave-one-participant-out sensitivity analysis without deletion.

## 10. Multiplicity and reporting

- Single primary H1 at alpha 0.05; single confirmatory secondary H2 labelled as secondary.
- Multi-task and alternative-measure results are exploratory, with family-wise Benjamini–Hochberg FDR.
- Effect sizes and 95% CIs are reported before p values.
- Non-significance is not described as evidence of equivalence or absence of effect.
- The flow of all analysis populations and reasons for algorithm exclusion are reported in tables.

## 12. Stopping rules

After the full analysis, continuation as an independent manuscript is reassessed if any of the following applies:

- Eligible N for the primary analysis < 50
- Algorithm success rate < 80% on either side in SP
- Sensor orientation inconsistent across sites so that left–right comparisons lack measurement validity
- An error in the clinical laterality definition or a data-use restriction is found

A null result is not itself a reason to stop; it would be considered as a scientific finding of clinical–sensor disagreement.

## 13. Change management

Changes after finalization are recorded as amendments stating the date, reason, whether results had been seen, and the expected impact.
